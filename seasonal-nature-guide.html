Learning to Notice the Seasons

From the first warm breeze to the changing colour of leaves, nature gives us countless quiet signs of time passing.

Nature 7 min read Evermere Journal

The seasons rarely arrive all at once. They announce themselves gradually through changes in light, temperature, colour, sound, and the small details of everyday life outdoors.

The first signs can be almost impossible to notice when life is moving quickly. A warmer breeze passes through an open window. A tree begins to develop tiny new leaves. Morning light stays a little longer.

Paying attention to these changes can make ordinary days feel more connected to the natural world.

Look for the first small changes

Seasonal change is often easier to notice when you stop looking for dramatic differences. Instead, pay attention to small shifts that appear almost quietly.

The colour of the sky, the temperature of the morning air, the sound of birds, and the appearance of new flowers can all become gentle markers of time.

Small signs worth noticing
  • The first noticeably warmer or cooler breeze.
  • New leaves appearing on familiar trees.
  • Changes in the length and quality of daylight.
  • Different birds, flowers, or sounds outdoors.

Let familiar places change

One of the easiest ways to notice the seasons is to return to the same outdoor places. A neighbourhood park, garden, walking route, or even a single tree can become a quiet calendar of natural change.

Visit the same place at different points throughout the year and the differences begin to reveal themselves. What looked bare months ago may become full and green, while later the same branches may carry warm autumn colours.

Familiarity makes these changes easier to see. You already know the shape of the place, so even subtle differences stand out.

Follow the changing light

Light is one of the most noticeable signs of the seasons once you begin paying attention. The angle of the morning sun, the length of an afternoon, and the colour of evening skies all shift throughout the year.

Even the same window can frame a completely different scene depending on the season. Watching these changes can bring a slower rhythm to ordinary mornings and evenings.

Nature does not rush from one season to the next. Its changes happen gradually, giving us plenty of quiet moments to notice them.

Notice seasonal sounds

Not every seasonal change is something you can see. Sounds shift too. Some mornings may bring different birdsong, stronger wind, rain against leaves, or the quieter atmosphere of a colder day.

Taking a short walk without headphones can make these details much easier to notice. What first seems like silence often contains layers of small sounds.

Let food mark the year

Seasonal change also appears at the table. Fresh fruit, vegetables, herbs, and other foods naturally become associated with different times of the year.

Paying attention to what is fresh and available can be another simple way to stay connected with the rhythm outside.

Create your own seasonal rituals

A seasonal ritual does not have to be elaborate. It might be an annual walk through the same park, an early morning coffee outdoors, or a habit of photographing the same tree throughout the year.

These small traditions create a personal record of time passing. They also encourage you to look more closely at changes that might otherwise disappear into the background.

Stay close to the rhythm

The seasons are always changing, whether we notice them or not. Taking time to observe their quieter signs can make everyday life feel a little more grounded.

A warm breeze, a new leaf, a longer evening, or the first hint of autumn colour may seem like a small thing. But together, these moments remind us that time is moving and nature is changing alongside us.
